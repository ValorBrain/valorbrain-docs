Numbers on this page come from the engine's benchmark register; the public mirror lives on the [product benchmarks page](https://valorbrain.com.br/benchmarks). They are retrieval-evidence numbers unless labeled otherwise — treating them as answer accuracy is a category error (see below).

## Two kinds of number

**Retrieval recall (R@K)** — did the right evidence land in the top-K? Measured against gold evidence, no LLM in the loop.

**Response accuracy** — did an LLM judge accept the answer? What commercial leaderboards usually publish.

Comparing ValorBrain's session-level recall@10 with someone else's judged accuracy is the most common wrong read of this page. When the engine publishes judged scores (BEAM via AMB), the label is average and binary.

## Reproducible register

| Benchmark | Metric | Result | Run |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LoCoMo** | session-level recall@10 | **96.58%** | 2026-07-29 — 1918/1986 questions, 10 conversations. Each conversation has 19–32 sessions; a hit means a session containing the evidence is among the top 10. Random baseline (10 sessions drawn at random, exact hypergeometric over the same 1986 questions): 42.25%. |
| **BEAM-100K** (AMB harness) | average 75.5% (binary 80.8%) | **75.5%** | 2026-08-23 — 20 conversations, 400 questions. Reader GLM-5.3 Flash (effort=max, not a product default — the reader is the customer's LLM), judge GLM-5.2. |
| **BEAM-10M** (AMB harness) | average 51.2% (binary 56.0%) | **51.2%** | 2026-08-24 — 10 conversations, 200 questions, same reader and judge. |

Context, not a head-to-head: on the same AMB scoreboard, Hindsight averages 64.1% on BEAM-10M with a different reader (Gemini 3.1 Pro) and judge (Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite).

Full numbers and method: [valorbrain.com.br/benchmarks](https://valorbrain.com.br/benchmarks). An older LoCoMo register was measured on a half-indexed corpus (documents counted before their vectors reached the hybrid index) and is not reproducible; the engine marks it *do not cite*.

## The pipeline behind the numbers

The 96.58% session-level recall@10 measures the whole production pipeline. Hybrid retrieval: BM25 (Portuguese and English) and dense vectors (LFM2.5-Embedding-350M, 1024 dimensions) fused by reciprocal-rank fusion, then a cross-encoder rerank (BGE-Reranker-v2-m3) and MMR for diversity. An LLM only expands the query; it never ranks. Personalized PageRank exists behind a flag and is off in production.

## Rules the engine holds itself to

These came from real, expensive mistakes; a benchmark that breaks them measures the harness, not the system.

1. **Wait for the index, not the embedding.** A document is searchable by the dense leg only after it lands in the hybrid index. Gates that waited on embedding state measured a half-indexed corpus and produced a non-reproducible register.
2. **Never benchmark against a moving system.** No restarts or deploys mid-run; no second benchmark sharing the BM25 statistics or the dedup budget.
3. **Fingerprint the corpus.** The corpus is alive; runs are comparable only under the same fingerprint.
4. **Know the noise floor before comparing.** On LoCoMo it is ±2 documents in 10 conversations; below that, a "regression" is weather.
5. **The judge is never the responder.** Answer-quality benchmarks use a judge model from a different family than the reader — self-preference bias is real and measurable.
6. **An empty reader response is a harness defect** — counted, not averaged away.

## Where the numbers come from

- Canonical public runs: the **AMB harness** (agent-memory-benchmark, `vectorize-io`), same harness the public scoreboard uses.
- Research iterations inside the engine (`beam-qa.ts`) use a different prompt/judge pair and are labeled *internal* — they are for choosing what to fix, never for publishing.

On an on-premise (Enterprise) installation, to measure your own instance: `bun run gate:locomo` (nightly recall floor, session-level recall@10 ≥ 0.90 default) and `bun run gate:beam` (average and binary via AMB) ship with the engine. They cost LLM tokens and time; they are gates, not unit tests.
